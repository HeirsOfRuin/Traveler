# Frame: the rest of the Norway visit

**Status: built, 2026-11-24.** The author read this plan and said "Go ahead".
The scenes are `content/scenes/norway/02-the-days.yaml`. What follows is the
plan as it was; "As built" at the foot says where the scenes differ.

It follows `STORY.md` (decided the same day) and the six beats of a visit.
The first night covers beats 1 to 5. This frame is the days after it, up to
the door: about 17 scenes, which with the 8 built makes the 25 the SPEC
estimated for a visit.

## What the visit has to do

1. Introduce the other three people (Hild, Finn, Eyvind), so the choice of
   where to wait is a choice between people met.
2. Let the hero use what they know twice more, with the presentation choice
   each time, in front of different people.
3. Show the debt up close: the ship, and what Thorgrim wants it for.
4. Find out what happened on the uncle's last night, by whichever road the
   first night opened.
5. Find the tide: the door will not stay open.
6. Leave through the door with a reason to come back.

## The days

Three days and nights. The tide gives the hurry: on the first morning Kol
reports that the "wet smell" on the hill is fainter than yesterday.

## What each choice adds

| Choice | The three roads | What it sets for later |
|---|---|---|
| Where to spend the day (10) | Finn / Hild / Hrapp | Which of the people the hero knows best, and which half of the uncle's story they have |
| A need (11) | Craft / open / explained | Standing, again, in front of a different audience from the first night |
| Eyvind's verse (13) | True / better / none | What other halls hear: the hero's name abroad |
| Counsel (14) | Useful / true / the question back | Whether Thorgrim hears the hero as Unkel again or as somebody else |
| What to tell Kol (16) | Far off / the truth / later | Whether the hall knows about the stone by the time the hero is gone |
| Surety (17) | The kit / a promise / a person's word | What the Norway life starts with, if it is the life chosen |

Every scene with a choice is written for all four occupations where the
occupation would change what the hero sees (10, 11a, 11b, 11c, 14).

## What will be measured

- The validator's three-roads rule on every choice.
- The same tests as the first prototype, extended: every one of the
  3 x 3 x 3 x ... ways reaches the door; looks and manner move nothing.
- A count of words read in one playthrough against the SPEC's estimate.
- Whether any hero can leave without having met all four people. None should.

## What is not known

- The history: what a keel being laid looked like, whether a healer would
  keep a tally in knots, how a hall argued a thing out. All from memory.
  Vikingr's research covers some of it and is to be read first.
- A woman's days in the hall. This frame is written without "he" or "she",
  and three of these scenes (the forge, the boat shed, the counsel) are
  places a woman of the era might not be asked into. That is a difference to
  write, not to hide: the plan is that she gets there by a different door
  each time, and the frame for that is owed before those scenes are written.
- Length. Three roads at each of six choices is a great deal of prose if
  nothing is shared. The plan shares the places and the people and varies who
  the hero is to them, as the first night did.

arrival), 8 choices of three roads where the plan had 6 (the need in each of
the three places counts once in the plan and is three scenes).

Found by a test while it was being written: the uncle's story on the last
night was keyed on "has this hero ever presented something as a craft", so a
hero who said nothing on the first night and then called the day's need a
craft read the wrong version. The first night's choice is now remembered by
name (`road.first`) and everything that follows from it reads that.
