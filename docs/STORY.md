# The story, and what each mechanic is for

**Status: a proposal, 2026-11-24, waiting on the author.** He asked: "Give me
more of the story frameworked and outlined to better tell how the story sits
and what each mechanic should add."

`SPEC.md` says what the game is. `DESIGN.md` says what is built. This page
says what happens, in what order, and why each system is in the game at all.
Everything decided is marked **decided** with where. Everything else is
proposed here for the first time and is his to strike.

Nothing on this page is written as scenes except the prologue and the first
night in Norway. History on it is from memory unless `HISTORY.md` holds it.

---

## 1. The story in one page

Your uncle Walter built a door. He is a man who cannot stop explaining
things, and behind the door are three places where explaining things gets a
person hurt.

He went through to each of them more than once (the boulder has nine marks).
In each he did some real good with what he knew, was not believed about why
it worked, made one promise too large to keep, took payment for it, and left
in a hurry. **Decided:** in Norway the good was the boiled water, the promise
was a ship that sails into the wind, and he left with a knife cut on his hand.

You follow him. For a few days the door opens easily and you are always one
place behind him (Act 1). Then it shuts, and will not open for four years
(**decided**, 16 seasons). You pick one of the three places to wait in, and
the wait turns into a life (Act 2): a place earned, a person you might love,
and your uncle, who is somewhere in that same country hiding from the people
he promised.

When the door opens again you are standing in front of it with whatever you
have made of the four years. The ending is read from four things
(**decided**): whether the uncle is beside you, the place you earned, who
else is beside you, and which side of the door you choose.

**What it is about.** Belonging. You arrive with knowledge nobody else has
and no place at all, and the knowledge is only worth something if it comes
out of your hands in a shape people can take. The uncle is the same person
without that lesson: every true thing he says costs him a little more. The
wistful part is that he wrote to you, and not to anybody else, because you
were the only one who ever let him finish.

### Three things proposed here that the story needs

| | Proposed | Why | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why the door shuts | **The door has a tide.** It opens easily for a few days at the spring of it, then ebbs for sixteen seasons. The smell of low tide in the shed is the first sign, and his notebook has the table | Gives the four years a reason inside the story, gives Act 1 its hurry, and makes the last season a thing everybody can see coming | The frame burns out and has to be rebuilt; or no reason is given |
| Where the uncle is in Act 2 | **Wherever you choose to wait.** The last clue of Act 1 shows he went back to put one of his promises right, and the era you pick is the one he picked | Every life has the uncle in it, and no era is the "right" one | He is in a fixed era, and two lives out of three are without him |
| What "saving" him means | **Making his promise good, or making it not matter,** and getting him to the door. Not a rescue from a dungeon | It turns the trail into the same lesson as the rest of the game: his debt is settled by doing, or by standing high enough that it is forgiven | A rescue |

## 2. The shape

```
PROLOGUE   The shed.                                   built
ACT 1      The chase. Three visits, a few days each.   Norway's first night built
             Norway, about 870
             Japan, the 1560s
             Rome and the bay, AD 79
           The door ebbs. Choose where to wait.
ACT 2      The life. Four years, sixteen seasons, in one era.
             Year 1  On sufferance
             Year 2  A use
             Year 3  A name
             Year 4  The door
ENDING     Go home, or stay.
```

A playthrough sees three visits and one life (**decided**). The other two
lives are a reason to play again.

### What a visit is (Act 1, about 25 scenes each)

Every visit has the same six beats, so the player learns the game's grammar
three times in three costumes:

| Beat | What happens | Norway, as built or planned |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The slow look | One long look at the place before anything goes wrong. This is the wonder | The fjord from the hill. **Built** |
| 2. Useless | The hero fails at something a child there can do. This is the comedy | The fire; the lintel; the basin; no spoon. **Built** |
| 3. His leaving | A thing the uncle taught that stuck, with the reason left out | Unkel's water, counted in twenties. **Built** |
| 4. His promise | What he owes, to whom, and what it will cost you | The ship into the wind; half a pound of silver. **Built** |
| 5. A need | Somebody is hurt or something is broken, the hero acts, and chooses how to present it | The smith's throat. **Built** |
| 6. The way on | A clue that says where he went, and the door | Not built. Proposed: what Hrapp's knife was about, and a second page of the notebook hidden in the forge |

A visit also introduces that era's four people (two men, two women), so that
the choice of where to wait is a choice between people the player has met.

### What a year is (Act 2, four seasons each, about 115 scenes for one era)

| Year | The question | What opens | What presses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. On sufferance | Will they keep you through the winter? | Work, learning their ways, the first needs | The keep: a stranger who does not earn is a burden |
| 2. A use | What are you to them? | Courting; the trail leaves the district; journeys | Talk about you has had a year to settle |
| 3. A name | Who are you here? | The uncle is found. His promise comes due | The era's own trouble arrives (below) |
| 4. The door | What will you take with you, or stay for? | The truth, told or not, to the one person | Losses are final this year (**decided**) |

A loss before year 4 ends a chapter and bends the story (**decided**): cast
out of the hall, the hero winters somewhere worse and comes back with less.

### Each era's own trouble (the backdrop; the hero is never in a real person's deeds)

| Era | The trouble | What it asks the household | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norway, 870s | A king is making the coast pay him. Halls are deciding whether to bend, fight, or sail west to empty land | Stay or go. This is why Thorgrim wants a ship that sails into the wind | The king and the dates are in `HISTORY.md`. The westward settlement in these same years is from memory and unchecked |
| Japan, 1560s | A small province between two larger lords, in the first years of guns | Whose side, and how soon | Unresearched. Not the first era built |
| Rome and the bay, AD 79 | A mountain nobody thinks of as a mountain | Nothing, until the last season | The day of the eruption is disputed (`HISTORY.md`). **Open:** do the four years end with it, or begin with it? |

## 3. The people

Four in each era, two men and two women (**decided**). The two of the sex the
hero courts can be courted; all four are in the story for every hero.

### Norway

| | Who | Wants from you | If not courted, they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aud Ketilsdóttir** | Thorgrim's widowed sister. The keys are hers. Reads people fast and says so. **Built** | Standing. She will not stand beside somebody the hall has not accepted | Your patron, and the one who tells you how the hall is taking you |
| **Hild** (proposed) | Gyda the healer's apprentice. Has noticed that Unkel's water works and wants to know why, which nobody else does | The truth, all of it. She is the one person explaining is right for | The one who can make what you know look like a craft |
| **Finn Ormsson** (proposed) | The boat-builder's son. Builds by eye and is better than his father | Standing, and proof you can make a thing | Your rival for the ship, or your partner in it |
| **Eyvind** (proposed) | A skald who goes between halls and carries their news | That you have been somewhere. He is bored by people who stay | The way stories about you travel, for good or ill |

Also built: **Thorgrim** (the hall is his, and the debt), **Kol** (finds
everything first), **Hrapp** the smith (does the uncle's voice; hated him;
you saved his life), **Gyda**, **Ulf**, **Orm** (each occupation's rival).

### Japan and Rome

Not cast. Each needs the same four seats: one who wants standing, one who
wants the truth, one who wants you to have made something, one who wants you
to have been somewhere. Names and stations wait on research.

## 4. What each mechanic is for

The test for every system: say in one line what the story loses without it.
If nothing, it goes.

| Mechanic | What the player does | What it adds to the story | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creation** | Makes a person: occupation, two traits, a look, a manner, who they would court | It is their story. The occupation decides what they know and who in each place already does that job and will resent them. Looks and manner change how they are written | Built |
| **The kit** | Carries one hand's worth through the door | Three ways to be a stranger: with a trade, with what he knew, with something to give. Sets how the first people take you | Built |
| **Presentation** | Each time they use what they know: as a craft, done and left alone, or explained | **This is the game.** Feeling clever, and choosing how clever to look. The uncle is what happens to someone who only has the third | Built for one scene |
| **Standing** (place, talk) | Watches two things move, in words | The scarce resource. Place is what the hall has given; talk is what it is saying. Too much talk for your place and they turn on you | Built and shown; nothing reads it |
| **Reading the room** | Guesses how much room there is, from how people act | The hall never tells you. A friend does. This is why bonds matter before romance does | Modelled (Gate B) |
| **A need** | Somebody is hurt, something is broken, and they are the one who could help | The temptation. Without it a careful player is never in danger and never a hero | Built for one scene |
| **Their ways** | Spends seasons learning how these people do things | Cover. The better they know the local way, the more of their own passes as craft. Also the comedy of being bad at it | Modelled |
| **The keep** | Works, or is a burden | Time pressure. Every season spent on the uncle or on love is a season not earning | Modelled |
| **Exposure, softly** | Is cast out, and comes back with less | The stakes, without ending the game. The harder road is still a road | Modelled |
| **Courtship and the truth** | Tends a bond; decides how much to tell, and when | The same choice as presentation, with one person. A bond stops short until the truth is told, and telling it early is its own risk | Modelled |
| **The trail** | Follows the uncle through clues | The plot. It needs journeys and it needs standing: nobody tells a stranger the rest | Modelled; first clues built |
| **Clues, drawn** | Finds things he wrote, labelled and cut | The uncle on the page before he is ever met. His hand, his habits, his worry. Kept in the Journal | Built: six, drawn |
| **His leavings** | Meets what he taught, gone slightly wrong | Comedy, and proof that the hall keeps what works and drops the reason | Built for one |
| **A journey** | Leaves the hall for a season | Wonder, the wider country, a place to be clever where nobody knows you, and something of the uncle every time | Modelled |
| **The dangerous way** | Takes a named risk for more ground | The only place the hero can die, and only by choosing it | Modelled |
| **The door's tide** | Watches the seasons run out | The clock. Room for two of the three things and not for all (measured: two fit at sixteen seasons, not twelve) | Modelled |
| **The deck** | Nothing | Nothing authored is read twice in one life | Not laid |

### What the measured numbers say the story must do

From `KILLGATE.md`, as constraints on the writing:

- Explaining everything ends finally exposed in about nine lives in ten. The
  explained road must **buy** something each time (in the hall it buys the
  truth about the uncle), or nobody will take it twice.
- A hero who presents everything as a craft is never exposed and earns a name
  in about one life in four. Safe has to be slow.
- A place plus a courtship is nearly certain (about 94%). **A courtship has to
  cost something besides seasons.** Proposed: each of the four people wants
  one thing that pulls against another goal (Aud wants standing, which the
  trail eats; Hild wants the truth, which is talk).

## 5. Clues

**Decided, 2026-11-24:** "having clues like notes, carvings, etc should have
some art to them".

A clue is anything written, labelled or cut by a person. It is drawn as the
object it is, in its own paper and ink, the same in every era, and the
Journal keeps each one found. Six are built:

| Clue | Where | What it tells |
|---|---|---|
| The letter | The shed | Who he is; that he writes to nobody else |
| The note on the bench | The shed | He meant to be back; he was bleeding when he wrote it |
| Two masking-tape labels | The shed | How much he trusts his own work |
| Nine marks on the boulder | The hill | He has done this nine times. You had thought once |
| A page of his notebook | The hill, if you carried it | The people, and the one thing he knows about himself |

Proposed for the rest of Norway: a rune stick he had Kol cut for him and got
wrong; a drawing in ash that somebody has kept under a turned bowl; a tally
on the forge wall in Hrapp's hand; the second half of the note.

Each era needs its own kinds. Proposed: Norway cuts (wood, stone, bone);
Japan brushes (paper, a wooden tag, a seal); Rome scratches and paints (wax
tablet, wall graffiti, a potsherd). None is drawn yet.

## 6. What this leaves for the author

1. **The tide.** Does the door have one? (Section 1.)
2. **The uncle is wherever you wait.** Yes, or a fixed era?
3. **What saving him means.** Making the promise good, or a rescue?
4. **The order of the visits.** Norway, Japan, Rome is assumed. The teacher's
   shelf in the shed already hints at all three.
5. **Rome's four years:** ending with the mountain, or beginning with it?
6. **The three proposed people in Norway** (Hild, Finn, Eyvind).
7. **What a courtship costs** besides time.
