# History: what was checked, and how far

The SPEC marked four things "from memory". This file is the first check of
them, done 2026-11-22 by web search. **The sources below are encyclopaedia
pages and press articles, not the scholarship itself.** That is enough to fix
a year for a sample scene and not enough to print. Each row says what a proper
check still needs.

A claim that is not in this file is from memory and is treated as unchecked.

## Japan, the 1560s

| Claim | Finding | Effect on the game |
|---|---|---|
| "Foreigners had been landing since the 1540s" | The first Portuguese reached Tanegashima in 1543 by the Japanese record, 1542 by the Portuguese. They came on what is variously called a Chinese junk or a Portuguese ship, and sold arquebuses. | Holds. A foreign face in the 1560s is rare and startling, and not unheard of, and it is tied in people's minds to guns and to the new religion. |
| "The one Japanese period where a nobody rose to rule" | Toyotomi Hideyoshi had no samurai lineage; his father is given as an ashigaru, a peasant foot soldier. He was in Oda Nobunaga's service by Okehazama (1560) and one of his leading generals by 1568. Little is known of his life before 1570. | Holds, with a caution: the early Hideyoshi stories are thin and late. The game's ladder can point at the fact of his rise and should not lean on its details. |

Still needed: everything. Japan is new ground (the SPEC says so) and is not
the first era built.

## Rome and the Bay of Naples, AD 79 to 80

| Claim | Finding | Effect on the game |
|---|---|---|
| Vesuvius in 79 | The year holds. **The day does not.** 24 August comes from Pliny the Younger's letters. A charcoal inscription found at Pompeii in 2018, read as 17 October, led to a proposed 24 October; autumn fruit in the ruins was cited with it. The Pompeii park later tested how long such charcoal lasts, concluded the inscription could be from October 78, and withdrew its support for the October date. August remains the official date and the question is open. | The game does not name the day. It can show the season by what is on the tables, and it must pick one season and keep it. This is a decision for the author. |
| The Colosseum opened in 80 | Titus opened it with games of about a hundred days. Sources give 80, and some 81. | "AD 79 to 80" holds as proposed. The opening is not given a month until checked. |

Still needed: the Pliny letters read directly (6.16 and 6.20); Dio and
Suetonius on the games; Imperator's notes for what carries from ten years
earlier.
